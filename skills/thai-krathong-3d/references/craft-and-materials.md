# Craft construction and material interpretation

See [research-evidence.md](research-evidence.md) for sources. Separate physical craft facts from modelling assumptions.

## Leaf preparation and assembly

DLTV's natural-material krathong lesson shows a stem base, prepared leaf strips with upper corners folded toward the centre, several folded pieces layered with offsets, attachment around the base, then flowers and candle/incense. One teaching example uses approximately 1.5 by 6 inch strips and groups three folds. These are example proportions, not universal rules.

Translate the construction into meshes:

1. Build a shallow stem disk with distinct pale fibrous cut face and outer side wall.
2. Cover the top with an actual leaf sheet. Orient grain and seams according to the reference; do not stretch a radial rim texture across the centre.
3. Define the centre ridge and side hinge lines of one folded unit. Build connected planar sections with thickness and consistent grain across folds.
4. Separate overlapping flaps enough for the crease to read. Add pins/staples only if visible or requested.
5. Repeat units around the selected footprint, offsetting consecutive rings. Preserve exposed floor and negative space.
6. Add flowers/accessories only after accepting the base and leaf silhouette. Decoration must not hide incorrect construction.

DLTV's four-corner-container lesson discusses cleaning along leaf strips and preparing pieces to size. This informs directional fibres, but does not make every food-container fold a floating krathong design.

## Named folds

The Silpakorn article confirms Hak-Kor-Ma as an established basic form. For ผกา, กุหลาบ, หัวขวาน, การเวก or another named pattern, obtain a pattern-specific diagram/demonstration before claiming its exact sequence. If evidence is incomplete, name the preset by visible construction and record ridge position, face angles, flap width, tip and overlap. A curved pointed blade is not interchangeable with a folded stack.

## Lotus

The morphology research distinguishes broad and narrow petals, motivating independent length/width controls. In the supplied digital references, model a pinched root, broad middle, transverse cup, longitudinal bend, restrained edge curl and distinct tip. Vary outer/middle/inner whorl pitch and radius. Add a dome, floor or opening only when supported by the selected screenshot. Thick lotus-candle forms may differ from fresh petals.

## Material families

| Family | Structure | Surface cues | Failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Banana leaf | thin folded sheet | directional grain, restrained wax sheen, crease shade | smooth green spikes |
| Banana stem | shallow disk | pale fibrous cut face and distinct side | uniform brown cylinder |
| Lotus/petal form | broad cupped shell | soft gradient, subtle veins | flat triangles or plastic spikes |
| Bread | flattened thick slices/rolls | porous crumb, browned crust | orange lotus without slice structure |
| Terracotta | hollow curved vessel | substantial rim, rough granular body | capped cylinder without interior |

A screenshot can leave wax/bread/petal identity uncertain; record that uncertainty. Surface colour alone cannot distinguish these constructions. Numerical shader settings are look-development choices, not measured physical properties.
