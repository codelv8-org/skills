# Art direction: semi-realistic AAA fantasy

Use realistic anatomy and plausible fabric/metal construction with selective stylization: elegant elongated silhouette, refined eyes, sculpted hair clumps, clean facial planes and high-quality material transitions. Do not make every character a rustic monk, a hyperreal soldier, or an anime chibi. High-status fantasy reads through tailoring, layered silk, intentional trim, restrained jewelry, and clear silhouette, not random gold or excessive emissive noise.

Treat references to named fictional characters as direction for an original design: extract broad genre cues and create a distinct face, crest, pattern and costume silhouette rather than copying a proprietary asset. Allow user-provided original style and all adult character genders, ages and body types. Choose an archetype based on request; record the choice, silhouette anchors, color/material inventory and intended viewing distance. Build front/side/back/three-quarter reference sheets or annotate supplied views. If image generation is unavailable, make a text brief and annotated reference crops, not a fabricated sheet.

## Feature inventory
- Head: cranial contour, brow/eye shape, jaw, nose, ears, hairline, hairstyle, facial age/style.
- Body: height, head units, clavicle/waist/hip widths, limb lengths, hand/foot size, stance.
- Costume: layer order, attachment positions, panels, collar overlap, sleeve width, hem length, left/right asymmetry, negative space.
- Hair: crown anchor, part line, front locks, side locks, ponytail/back mass and profile depth.
- Ornament: major plate forms, edge trim, motif scale, repeated shapes, gold coverage, gem settings and connection points.
- Materials: swatches under neutral lighting, fabric weave direction, metal and stone finish, surface roughness ranges. Mark hidden areas as inferred; keep consistent identity across views.

## Visual review
At locked ortho cameras compare actual gray clay views and crops against the interpretation. Use shared framing and scale, note perspective differences and never alter reference to improve scores. Compare silhouette overlay/landmarks only as diagnostic. Evaluate major silhouette first, secondary forms second, fine detail last. Log severity, part, view, discrepancy and concrete fix. Any critical form, silhouette or costume hierarchy failure blocks next gate. Keep approved screenshots and variants; high quality means actual results, not prompt quality.
